MyVector — executive benchmark summary

Native vector search (HNSW) for MySQL, benchmarked head-to-head vs vsql_vector, pgvector & MariaDB · 2026-11-08 20:56 UTC

fair same-host · cross-engine · by version · glove 1.2M · neutral harness · x86 / cross-ISA

Bottom line: after two shipped optimizations, MyVector's HNSW is competitive-to-leading at matched recall against VillageSQL's vsql_vector, pgvector, and MariaDB's native vector index — on real ann-benchmarks datasets scored against published ground truth, measured on the same hardware and, for cross-checking, through a neutral third-party harness. The results below are reported with their caveats intact, including where MyVector still trails.

The four takeaways

#FindingEvidence
1MyVector leads or ties at high recall on the same host. At recall 0.999 it serves the most QPS of the four engines; it wins outright at recall 1.0./fair/ (A1, same-host 4-way)
2It scales where others don't. On glove-100 (1.18M, cosine) MyVector builds in ~46 min and dominates matched-recall QPS; vsql's build is super-linear (~7 h) and MariaDB cannot build the index within 10 h (measured, excluded)./glove/
3The win reproduces in a neutral harness. The same recall/QPS advantage shows up through VillageSQL's own vector-dev-bench, not just MyVector's harness — so it isn't a measurement artifact./village/
4The speedups are hardware-general. The vectorized distance kernels reproduce on AMD (9.4×), Intel (4.2×) and ARM/NEON (3.8×) silicon./x86/

What we tested

  • Engines: MyVector (MySQL 8.4/9.7/26.10 component), VillageSQL vsql_vector, pgvector (Postgres 17), MariaDB 12.3 native vector index.
  • Datasets: fashion-mnist-784-euclidean (60k×784, L2), nytimes-256-angular (290k×256, cosine), glove-100-angular (1.18M×100, cosine) — ann-benchmarks HDF5, recall@10 vs the datasets' published exact neighbors.
  • Method: HNSW M=16, ef_construction=200 (where tunable); server-side query timing; recall/throughput ef_search sweeps; engine-isolated or same-host as each page states; two harnesses (MyVector's and VillageSQL's neutral one) for cross-validation.
  • Hardware: Ampere A1/A2 (arm64) for the engine comparisons; AMD EPYC & Intel Xeon (x86) via CI runners for the cross-ISA kernel validation.

Honest caveats (the unbiased part)

  • Where MyVector still trails: at the very lowest ef_search operating points, per-query execution overhead leaves pgvector ahead on raw QPS. PR #280 cut most of it; the remainder is architectural (RFC #281) plus MySQL's inherent text-literal lexing, which every engine pays equally in these tests.
  • MariaDB on glove-100 is excluded — not omitted, but measured: its online MHNSW build did not complete within a 10 h cap at 1.18M rows (insert rate decays as the graph grows). It appears on the smaller fashion-mnist pages where its build does complete.
  • Neutral-harness scope: the /village/ page deliberately shows only what one tool measures for all four engines (recall/QPS/build); insert and exact-KNN live on the per-engine pages, to keep that comparison apples-to-apples.
  • x86 end-to-end numbers come from shared CI runners (indicative throughput, valid same-runner kernel A/B); a persistent dedicated x86 host for a same-host end-to-end A/B was out of scope on cost.
